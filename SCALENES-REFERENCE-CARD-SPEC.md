## Canonical structural anatomy

### Anterior scalene
- Origin: anterior tubercles of transverse processes C3–C6.
- Insertion: scalene tubercle on superior surface of first rib.
- Key relationship: phrenic nerve courses on the muscle surface; brachial plexus is posterior to the muscle in the interscalene passage.

### Middle scalene
- Origin: cervical transverse processes across the lower six cervical levels. Exact attachment pattern/upper extent varies between descriptions and individuals and should not be oversimplified in artwork.
- Insertion: superior surface of first rib posterior to the subclavian artery groove.
- Key relationship: forms posterior boundary of the interscalene passage; brachial plexus roots/trunks pass between anterior and middle scalenes.

### Posterior scalene
- Origin: posterior tubercles of lower cervical transverse processes, commonly described across the lower three or four cervical vertebrae.
- Insertion: anterior/external surface of second rib.
- Key relationship: long thoracic nerve course in the middle/posterior scalene region is variable.

## Innervation

Display concise card fact:

**Anterior rami of cervical spinal nerves, commonly described across C3–C8.**

Do not imply that the visible brachial plexus is the motor innervation of the scalenes merely because it is shown spatially near them.

## Neural / vascular visual policy for this card

### Primary visual — show
- Brachial plexus roots/trunks between anterior and middle scalenes.
- First rib and second rib.
- Cervical transverse-process attachment region.
- Clavicle as an orienting landmark if it does not obscure the rib insertions.

### Show if legible
- Subclavian artery through the interscalene triangle.

### Optional deeper/inset view
- Phrenic nerve on anterior scalene.
- Long thoracic nerve near/through the middle-posterior scalene region, with variability language.

Do not turn the primary card into a nerve atlas. Muscles remain visually dominant.

## Main illustration angle

Preferred main view: **anterior-oblique / three-quarter view**.

Reason:
- exposes cervical attachments,
- makes first- and second-rib insertions understandable,
- allows brachial plexus to be shown between anterior and middle scalenes,
- leaves room for the reciprocal referred-pain inset,
- preserves the visual language of the approved card without requiring a full side view.

A small second angle or attachment close-up is allowed if the clavicle or first rib obscures a key fact.
